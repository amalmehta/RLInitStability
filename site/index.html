Initialization and training stability in deep RL

Reinforcement learning keeps changing its own training targets, which makes it much harder on neural networks than supervised learning. This site collects what the research says about how a network's starting point and its later health decide whether training stays stable. It also lets you watch it happen in a PPO agent that trains live in your browser.

Seven things the literature agrees on

  1. Scale weights to the layer's width and activation. Variance of about 1/fan-in (LeCun), 2/(fan-in + fan-out) (Xavier) or 2/fan-in for ReLU (He) keeps signals from vanishing or exploding with depth. Orthogonal matrices do this exactly for linear networks. He et al. 2015, Saxe et al. 2014, Schoenholz et al. 2017.
  2. Initialize the policy head small. Shrinking the last policy layer's weights about 100x so the initial policy is near-uniform was one of the most robust recommendations in a study of more than 250,000 PPO agents. Andrychowicz et al. 2021, Huang et al. 2022.
  3. "Implementation details" are often the real algorithm. Orthogonal init, gradient clipping and advantage normalization explain much of PPO's edge over TRPO, and results vary sharply with seed. Engstrom et al. 2020, Henderson et al. 2018.
  4. Networks lose plasticity as they train on RL's moving targets. Symptoms include dormant neurons, collapsing feature rank, growing weight norms and sharpening curvature. No single metric explains it all. Sokar et al. 2023, Kumar et al. 2021, Lyle et al. 2024.
  5. Going back toward initialization helps. Resetting layers, shrink-and-perturb, recycling dormant units and regularizing toward the initial weights all restore plasticity. In this setting the init is a resource, not just a starting point. Nikishin et al. 2022, Ash and Adams 2020, Kumar et al. 2023.
  6. Normalization is now the main stabilizer. LayerNorm, together with norm control on weights, prevents many failures that resets and target networks used to patch, and lets RL networks scale up. Gallici et al. 2025, Lee et al. 2025, Nauman et al. 2024.
  7. Judge stability across seeds, with intervals. A single training curve says little. Report robust aggregates with confidence intervals over many runs. Agarwal et al. 2021.
What the live lab shows honestly: CartPole is easy, so with a shallow network most inits eventually reach the maximum return of 500. The differences show up first in the network's health: initial policy entropy, saturated or dormant units, weight norm and feature rank. Switch to ReLU to see init change the learning curve too: with N(0, 1) weights about half the units start dormant, the policy starts almost deterministic, and early learning lags.
